# Abstract, keywords and submission materials

## Title

When the Last Supplier Leaves: Supplier Loss, Absent Market Repair, and Access in US Generic Drug Markets

Alternative, shorter: Markets Do Not Repair Themselves: Supplier Loss in Sole-Source Generic Drug Markets

## Structured abstract (248 words)

**Objectives.** US generic drug markets are concentrated, and many ingredient and dosage-form
markets are served by a single manufacturer. When a supplier stops supplying, economic theory
expects replacement entry and clinical practice expects therapeutic substitution. Neither
mechanism has been tested at national scale. This study measures what happens to dispensing
after a supplier leaves, and whether either repair mechanism operates.

**Methods.** Medicaid State Drug Utilization Data for 2016Q1 to 2026Q1 were combined with the
FDA National Drug Code Directory and the openFDA bulk NDC dataset to define 3,258 prescription
drug markets at the ingredient by dosage-form level. A supplier-loss event was defined as a
labeler dispensing at least 200 prescriptions per quarter falling below 5% of that rate, giving
398 events. Events were stratified by the departing supplier's prior market share. Outcomes were
complete cessation of dispensing and the change in log dispensing over six quarters, measured
against a pre-wind-down baseline and differenced against 1,272 never-exposed markets with
placebo event dates. Inference was by permutation.

**Results.** Where the departing supplier held under 35% of the market, 0.8% of markets lost
supply. Where it was the sole supplier, 54.2% did (versus 0.2% of controls, p=0.0017). Results
were unchanged after excluding markets with contemporaneous FDA recalls. Replacement entry
occurred in 18% of sole-supplier markets versus 68% of minor-supplier markets. Dispensing of
other drugs in the same pharmacologic class rose 14.6%, against a 99% decline in the affected
market.

**Conclusions.** Markets do not repair sole-source supplier loss. Supplier count is observable
in advance, so the at-risk set is identifiable before failure.

## Cover letter

Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "When the Last Supplier Leaves: Supplier Loss, Absent
Market Repair, and Access in US Generic Drug Markets," for publication in Value in Health.

Research on pharmaceutical supply reliability concentrates on predicting and explaining
shortages. The market event that produces many of them, a manufacturer ceasing supply, has not
been studied for what follows it. Two repair mechanisms are usually assumed: replacement entry
by other manufacturers, and therapeutic substitution within the drug class. We test both
directly.

Using eleven years of national Medicaid dispensing records linked to the FDA National Drug Code
Directory, we identify 398 supplier-loss events across 3,258 ingredient and dosage-form markets
and stratify them by the departing supplier's prior market share. Losing a supplier that held
under 35% of a market produces no detectable effect on dispensing. Losing the sole supplier
causes complete cessation in 54% of markets, against 0.2% of 1,272 never-exposed control
markets. Replacement entry occurs in 18% of sole-supplier markets, and the therapeutic class
absorbs roughly 15% of lost volume against a 99% decline in the affected market.

The policy implication is specific and actionable. The determinant of harm is the number of
remaining suppliers, which is observable in public data before any failure occurs. Resilience
policy keyed to shortage status necessarily arrives after supply has already stopped.

All data are public, and all analysis code and intermediate results are available in a
repository that will be made public on acceptance. The manuscript is not under consideration
elsewhere. Neither author has a conflict of interest to declare, and the work received no
funding.

Thank you for your consideration.
